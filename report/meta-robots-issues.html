Critical Issue

Meta Robots & Indexing

Category pages lack proper meta robots directives, causing potential indexation issues and wasted crawl budget.

Site-Specific Observations

Issue Priority Rationale
Category Page Noindex Critical Category pages should be set to noindex to prevent thin-content pages from appearing in search results and diluting the site's overall quality signal.
Robots Meta Directive Missing Critical Category pages are missing the `noindex, nofollow` meta robots directive. Without this, search engines may index low-value archive pages.
Category Page noindex, follow Warning Even when noindex is applied, ensure the directive allows crawling (`follow`) so link equity can still flow from category pages to individual posts.

Understanding the Differences

noindex, nofollow

Full Block

Tells search engines not to index the page AND not to follow any links on it. Use this for low-value pages like internal search results, tag archives, or thank-you pages where you want to completely exclude everything.

noindex, follow

Recommended for Category Pages

Prevents the category page itself from appearing in search results but allows search engines to follow the links on the page. This preserves link equity flow from category pages to your individual article pages, which is the ideal approach for blog category archives.

Recommendations

1

Apply noindex, follow to All Category Pages

Add the following meta tag to every category archive page: <meta name="robots" content="noindex, follow">. This prevents indexation while preserving crawl flow to individual articles.

2

Remove Category Pages from Sitemap

Ensure noindexed category pages are also removed from the XML sitemap. Sending mixed signals (including in sitemap while setting noindex) can confuse search engines.

3

Audit Existing Indexed Category Pages

Check Google Search Console for currently indexed category pages. Once the noindex directive is in place, use the URL Removal Tool to request removal of any category pages already in the index.
